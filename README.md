# Moje parcele · Teren

Phone-first parcel field map. Open `/teren.html` (the root redirects there).

## Capabilities
- Exact parcel search against the public GeoSrbija search service, filtering parcel number, cadastral municipality, and municipality; EPSG:32634 conversion to WGS84 and back. Opening the search (or focusing the number field) with both place fields empty fills them from the parcel on the map (its KO resolved through `public/ko-ids.txt`, diacritics from the GeoSrbija description), otherwise from the last successful search, so usually only the number is typed; a note under the fields says where the place came from. The municipality and cadastral municipality fields suggest names from `public/ko-ids.txt` (a known municipality narrows the KO list; a KO found in only one municipality fills it in); free text still works. The table has no diacritics, so the result's names are taken from the GeoSrbija description ("Grošnica I · Kragujevac") when they match what was entered.
- Parcels for the whole map view (`public/area-loader.js`): once the map shows a real place (a precise GPS fix, an opened or saved parcel, a pin, the saved-parcels overview), moving or zooming it loads the parcels it shows, with no long press. The UTM 34N plane is cut into fixed 250 m cells; when the map rests for 300 ms each missing cell is fetched once through the circle that encloses it (radius 177 m, the same public `circle` query), three at a time, nearest to the centre first, with a tenth of the view loaded ahead on each side. Panning back costs no request. Beyond 36 cells (about 1.5 × 1.5 km, reached two zoom steps out from the GPS view on a phone) nothing is downloaded and the map asks to zoom in. Cells are kept on the device (IndexedDB store `cells`, database version 3, the 300 most recent downloads, 30–150 kB each), so a revisited area shows at once, also offline or while GeoSrbija is down; a copy older than seven days is shown and refreshed in the background. A failed cell is retried after 20 s or at once with "Pokušaj ponovo"; a cell query that hangs fails after 15 s. All parcels in view are drawn as one path and those off screen are skipped, so thousands of parcels stay smooth.
- Status line over the map: loading (with a spinner), "zoom in", "offline, only parcels loaded earlier", "GeoSrbija is not answering" with a retry button, and on phones the app's messages, which otherwise go to the panel hidden behind the map (long ones shortened to their first sentence; the panel keeps the whole text).
- Parcels around the phone: every parcel within 150 m of the GPS fix drawn as vector outlines with numbers; tap one to select it. The map location button (◎) always returns to this mode, including after a searched or saved parcel in another area; that parcel stays selected, and "Prikaži parcelu" goes back to it and its neighbors. This reuses the spatial `circle` request the GeoSrbija map viewer itself sends on a click (`st:"circle", s:"E,N,R", layers:"586,587,588,589,899,939,"`), so it needs no token or account. The place line for such parcels is the Latin part of the service's `desc` (cadastral municipality and municipality together); Vojvodina descriptions list the Cyrillic name first and roman numerals appear in both scripts, which the extraction handles.
- Share: "Podeli" sends `/teren.html?p=<number>&lat=&lon=` (a point inside the parcel, or one of its vertices for odd shapes) through the phone's share sheet, or copies it. Opening the link runs the circle query at that point, selects the parcel by number and loads its neighbors, without GPS or a KO lookup.
- Opening a searched or saved parcel also loads its neighboring boundaries, centered on the parcel geometry without needing GPS. The query covers the parcel bounding circle plus 150 m, capped at the service limit of 1 km with an explicit partial-coverage label for larger parcels. Clearing selection retains this area; the location button returns to GPS. Late responses cannot replace another area.
- Terrain packages include flat GeoSrbija records for neighboring parcels, so they remain selectable offline and travel with JSON backups. Opening an older saved parcel online fills this field automatically without recreating a package deleted during download. Failed refreshes retain cached boundaries.
- Satellite / map toggle (remembered per device): Esri World Imagery XYZ tiles under the parcel outlines (imagery dated March–April 2025 at the Pepeljevac and Belgrade reference points, versus 2020–2021 for the GeoSrbija orthophoto). Where no imagery exists at a zoom (rural areas above z18), the parent tile is scaled up. Imagery needs the network; offline the vector map is shown. Tiles come from ArcGIS Location Platform (`ibasemaps-api.arcgis.com`) with the key in `public/config.js`: 2 million free tiles a month; without pay-as-you-go, imagery stops until the next month once that is used up. The key is visible to every browser, so it must be restricted to the app domain in the ArcGIS portal, and it expires at most a year after creation.
- Owner data: eKatastar has no public API. Owner search needs an RGZ registered-user contract, and the public view requires a captcha, which the app does not bypass. "Vlasnik i list nepokretnosti" copies the parcel number and opens eKatastar `FindParcela.aspx?KoID=<KO number>`, where the municipality and cadastral municipality are already selected; the user pastes the number and types the captcha. The KO number comes from `public/ko-ids.txt` (built by `scripts/build-ko-ids.mjs` from the RGZ list of cadastral municipalities), matched against the GeoSrbija description. An ambiguous or unknown KO opens the generic eKatastar page instead.
- Canvas vector map with polygon holes, vertices, pan, scale, north reference. Pinch and wheel zoom keep the point between the fingers or under the cursor in place, two fingers also pan, and redraws are batched per animation frame. A quick tap selects a parcel or clears the selection and never discards loaded parcels. Moving the map loads parcels by itself (see above); a long press (550 ms; Android's long-press context menu and a desktop right-click do the same) still drops a pin and loads the parcels around it, which is how an empty map starts anywhere without GPS or search.
- User-initiated download of a bounded OpenStreetMap extract (~1 km around parcel center) through the app's own `/api/surroundings` Worker route: roads, tracks, buildings, land use, water. ODbL attribution links remain visible. No standard OSM tile bulk download.
- Device-local IndexedDB parcels and surroundings, service-worker app shell cache with explicit completeness check. App never reports full offline readiness based on geometry alone. Offline, the app opens the parcel shown last (else the first saved one); with no saved parcel and location already allowed it starts GPS, so parcels loaded on earlier visits show around the phone.
- Updates and installation: the new service worker takes over at once, and an open page then shows "Nova verzija aplikacije je spremna · Osveži" (not on the first install). Where the browser offers installation (`beforeinstallprompt`, Chrome and Edge), a "↓ Instaliraj" button appears in the header; on phones it replaces "Na telefon ↗", whose help stays in the bottom bar. No browser lets a site add itself to the home screen: Chrome opens its install dialog after a tap ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/How_to/Trigger_install_prompt)), Safari only from its menu (iOS 26: ••• → Share → Add to Home Screen, with Open as Web App on; [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-am/guide/iphone/iph42ab2f3a7/26/ios/26)). So once a phone has saved parcels and the app is not opened from the home screen, it suggests it: after "Sačuvaj za teren", after an import, when Chrome offers installation, and at start, at most every 14 days. On Android the suggestion's button opens Chrome's install dialog, and the saved parcels are there too; on an iPhone it shows the steps and "Izvezi sve sada", because the home-screen app keeps its data apart from Safari.
- Foreground high-accuracy geolocation, accuracy circle, nearest-boundary distance, inside/outside/uncertain status; stale fixes identified after 30 seconds or GPS stop.
- Location permission (`public/location-permission.js`): the app never asks on opening, since prompts without a tap are allowed far less often and Chrome stops showing them for a week after three dismissals. It reads the permission state (Permissions API): when location is already allowed, GPS starts by itself; otherwise a card on the empty map says why location helps, offers the button and points to search and long press. In Chrome 144+ that button is the browser's own `<geolocation>` element, which also lets a user who blocked the site allow it again without the site settings; elsewhere it is a normal button that calls the Geolocation API. Once location is blocked, the card shows the steps for the phone (Safari on iPhone, a home-screen app, Android, desktop), also over an open parcel, and GPS starts by itself when the permission comes back (change event, or a new check when the page is shown again). Safari on iPhone allows location for one visit unless the site is set to Allow, so iPhone users get that tip in the card and in the help. A website cannot open these settings or grant itself location.
- My parcels: a parcel can get its own name and one of six colours, stored only on this device. Naming an unsaved parcel stores it (boundary only, nothing is downloaded), and a terrain save that was already running keeps names edited meanwhile. A parcel can also get a group (free text; existing groups are suggested, and case or diacritics do not split a group). The saved list groups parcels by place or by these own groups, with each group's count and area and the total; the choice is remembered, and until one is made own groups are shown once there are any. A group heading fits that group on the map, "Sve moje parcele na mapi" fits all of them, and saved parcels stay visible and selectable in every map mode (a dot marks them when zoomed far out).
- Own note, points and photos per parcel, stored only on this device. Points (entrance from the road, gate, boundary stone, well, other) are placed under a fixed crosshair by moving the map, or at a fresh GPS fix; an entrance or gate becomes the suggested navigation destination. Photos are shrunk to 1600 px and kept in their own IndexedDB store (database version 2), so listing parcels never loads image data; a photo taken in the last two minutes gets the current GPS fix and appears on the map. Deleting a parcel deletes its photos in the same transaction. A single parcel's "Izvezi" file carries name, group, note and points, not photos; the full backup below includes them.
- Parcel details show the perimeter (total boundary length, enclaves included) and every side length from the projected UTM 34N rings; zoomed in, the map labels the sides.
- Walking guidance ("Vodi me pešice do tačke", works offline): a large arrow and the live distance to the chosen point, relative to the top of the phone when a compass is available (iOS asks for permission on that tap; Android uses absolute orientation), otherwise to north; a heading cone on the GPS dot and a dashed line to the target; arrows step through the points. The screen stays on (Screen Wake Lock, taken again when the page returns). Where the browser supports vibration, the phone vibrates on entering or leaving the parcel, counted only once the fix is clear of the boundary by more than its accuracy, and on arrival within max(3 m, accuracy). It is an orientation aid, not a way to stake out a boundary.
- External road navigation to a boundary vertex: Google Maps, Apple Maps, Waze, and on Android a `geo:` link that opens any installed map app, including offline ones such as OsmAnd or Organic Maps. The suggested vertex is the one nearest to a drivable road or track in the downloaded OSM surroundings (footpaths do not count); without surroundings it is T1, and a point the user picks stays picked. Offline straight-line bearing/distance. No offline road-routing engine or background tracking.
- Backup (`public/backup.js`): "Izvezi sve" writes one JSON file, `moje-parcele-YYYY-MM-DD.json`, with every saved parcel, the user's name, colour, group, note and points, and the photos as base64. Downloaded roads and neighbouring parcels are left out, since they make up most of the size; they are downloaded again when a parcel is opened online or saved for the field. "Uvezi iz fajla" takes a backup or a single exported parcel. A backup is stored at once, in one IndexedDB transaction: new parcels are added, while a parcel already on the phone keeps its own data and only gets empty fields filled and missing points and photos added, so importing the same file twice adds nothing. Every parcel's official polygon geometry is validated before use. Browser storage belongs to one site on one device, so the backup is also how saved parcels move to another phone or to another address of the app.

## Data provenance
The app ships with no built-in parcel. On online startup it starts location only when the browser already allows it for the site; otherwise a card on the empty map asks first (see Location permission). Once GPS is on, it automatically loads actual cadastral polygons within 150 m of the first fresh GPS fix with reported accuracy of 100 m or better (an overview threshold, not surveying accuracy). Coarse or stale locations do not trigger a parcel query; a better fix automatically resumes it. It refreshes after movement of 75 m, at most once per 30 seconds, with one request in flight. Tapping a polygon selects it; manual search and saved-parcel selection stop automatic nearby updates. Offline startup opens a saved parcel. All six regional cadastral parcel layers are queried: 586 (west and central Serbia: Pepeljevac, Čačak, Užice, Šabac), 587 (Kragujevac, Kraljevo, Kruševac, Novi Pazar), 588 (Niš, Zaječar, Bor, Požarevac), 589 (Leskovac, Vranje, Pirot), 899 (Vojvodina) and 939 (Belgrade, Smederevo). Layers 49 (settlement outlines) and 910 (addresses) answer the same query and are excluded. An empty response is reported as an empty query result, never as proof of missing parcels or missing digitization. Spatial searches ask for pages of 500 records (the service answered pages of up to 1,000 on 2026-09-29), stop at 1,000 records and label an incomplete result explicitly; a 150 m circle or a map cell needs one request even in a town centre. Every boundary comes from a live GeoSrbija search and carries its retrieval date. Nearby boundaries around a searched or saved parcel are loaded on open and saved with terrain packages; existing packages are enriched on online open. Public upstream availability is not guaranteed. `tests/fixtures/parcel.json` is a real search result (1227/2 Pepeljevac, Lajkovac, retrieved 2026-09-16) used only by the geometry tests.

Location fixes stay in page memory; there is no tracking history. Page views are counted with Vercel Web Analytics (the `/_vercel/insights/script.js` tag in `teren.html`, active once Web Analytics is enabled for the Vercel project). According to Vercel it uses no third-party cookies and counts visitors by a hash of the request that is discarded after 24 hours. It would also record query parameters, so the query string is removed before a view is sent: for a shared link it holds the parcel number and a point. For GPS nearby mode, the current search center is sent to GeoSrbija after location permission is granted. For a searched or saved parcel, its geometry determines the query center; this does not require phone location permission. External navigation uses only the selected destination in the URL. Parcel searches go from the browser to GeoSrbija (its servers were observed to time out for datacenter IPs, so they are not proxied; `GEOSRBIJA_URL` in `public/config.js` lets a deployment point at its own proxy). Surroundings go to the app's own Worker route, which forwards only a bounded query to Overpass.

## Surroundings service
`app/api/surroundings/route.ts` (`GET /api/surroundings?lat=&lon=`) is the only place that talks to Overpass. It validates that the centre lies in Serbia, rounds it to ~100 m, builds the fixed query from `lib/surroundings.js`, and returns `{elements, bbox, downloadedAt, source}`. Responses carry `Cache-Control: public, max-age=86400, s-maxage=604800` so the hosting CDN shares one download between users for 7 days (the browser also rounds the centre to 3 decimals so the URLs match); on Cloudflare the Workers Cache API is used in addition. Requests with `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site` are refused so other sites cannot use the deployment as a relay.

`OVERPASS_URL` (environment variable, comma-separated) lists the upstreams; the default is the public `overpass-api.de` followed by two public mirrors. Public instances are randomly busy (the same query answers in 3 s or returns 504), so the route hedges: each next upstream is started 5 s after the previous one, a busy 429/504 answer is retried once after 3 s, the first good answer wins and the others are aborted, all within a 50 s budget. Buildings are requested only within ~500 m of the parcel (roads, water, woods and land use within ~1 km) to keep town packages around 3 MB instead of 7 MB.

This only softens the problem. Production should run its own Overpass instance with the Serbia extract and list it first in `OVERPASS_URL`; `deploy/overpass/` contains a ready docker-compose (Overpass + Caddy HTTPS, protected by the `OVERPASS_KEY` header the route sends when the variable is set) and step-by-step instructions. For per-user limits use a rate-limiting rule of the hosting platform on `/api/surroundings`; the route itself has none.

`public/config.js` holds the browser-side setting `SURROUNDINGS_URL` (default `/api/surroundings`).

## Offline use
Install via Safari Add to Home Screen / Android install, open from installed icon online, save each parcel, wait for confirmed shell and data storage. Verify reopening in airplane mode before going into the field. Browser storage can be evicted or cleared; export a backup. GPS must be enabled and have a signal. Background updates while the app is closed are not implemented.

## Validation
`pnpm test` (or `node --test "tests/*.test.mjs"`, quoted so Node expands the pattern in any shell): nearest segment distance, holes, multipolygons, bearings, fixture coordinate/area, no built-in parcel, surroundings service validation and query, app shell completeness, offline navigation and asset fetch behavior, API exclusions.
Live managed-browser checks: successful GeoSrbija search; Overpass download; device storage persists through page reload; map renders actual parcel and surrounding roads. Managed HTTP preview does not permit a service worker or WebMCP: physical-phone GPS, installed PWA cold start in airplane mode, and WebMCP runtime validation remain unverified. Do not describe these as device-tested.

## Deployment
The project is a standard Next.js app: `pnpm build` runs `next build`, so importing the repository into Vercel works with the defaults (framework Next.js, output `.next`). Static frontend files in `public/` are self-contained and `app/page.tsx` provides the root redirect. Set the `OVERPASS_URL` environment variable in the hosting dashboard when you have your own Overpass instance; the `/api/surroundings` function declares `maxDuration = 60` because public Overpass instances can take 20–50 s to answer.

The original Sites/Cloudflare Worker packaging remains available as `pnpm dev:cloudflare`, `pnpm build:cloudflare` and `pnpm start:cloudflare` (vinext + wrangler). The API route reads settings from `process.env` and uses the Workers cache only when it exists, so the same code runs on both hosts.

## Storage on the phone and how long it lasts (measured 2026-09-29)
What the app stores, measured in desktop Chromium against live GeoSrbija:
- App files (service worker cache): 0.24 MB.
- Map areas (250 × 250 m): 20–135 kB each in the form Chrome stores them (V8 serialization, nine places from central Belgrade and Novi Sad to Vojvodina fields; a mountain point returned no parcels). 79 areas of central Belgrade took about 5 MB of IndexedDB on disk, so the 300-area limit stays near 20 MB even in a town centre (about 40 MB uncompressed). A phone screen at the GPS zoom (about 12 areas) is roughly 0.7 MB in central Belgrade and about a third of that in a village such as Pepeljevac, going by the measured area sizes.
- A saved parcel: its boundary alone about 6 kB; "Sačuvaj za teren" adds roads (3.2 MB for central Belgrade, measured when the building limit was added; less in a village) and up to 1,000 neighbouring parcels (0.6–1 MB).
- Photos are shrunk to 1600 px, JPEG 0.82: five landscape photos stored 41–139 kB each.

The app itself deletes only map areas beyond the 300 most recent downloads; an area older than seven days is still shown and refreshed when seen online. Saved parcels, notes and photos stay until the user deletes them, unless the browser removes the whole site: Safari after seven days of use without a visit to it ([WebKit](https://webkit.org/tracking-prevention/)), except a home-screen app, whose data is kept apart from Safari's; any browser when the device runs low on space, least recently used sites first, unless the site holds persistent storage ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)); clearing browser data; private browsing. Quotas are not the limit: Chrome and Safari 17 allow an origin up to about 60% of the disk ([WebKit storage policy](https://webkit.org/blog/14403/updates-to-storage-policy/)). Persistent storage is granted silently by Chrome and Safari according to their own rules, for example to home-screen apps; the test Chromium (not installed) denied it.

What the app does about it: the "Rezervna kopija i prostor" block in the saved list shows the space used, how many map areas are kept, whether the browser may delete the data, and the date of the last "Izvezi sve"; Safari on an iPhone gets a warning with the steps to the home screen and to moving the data there (export, then import), and the same note follows a save; the home-screen suggestion described under "Updates and installation" asks at the moments that matter. "Obriši parcele učitane sa mape" frees the map areas and keeps everything saved. Persistent storage is requested on every save and, when the app is opened from the home screen with saved parcels, at start. The only full protection against losing a phone or its data is a copy off the phone: today the backup file, later an account.

## Own copy of all parcels (not built, and why)
Serbia has 19,012,730 cadastral parcels ([eKatastar statistics](https://katastar.rgz.gov.rs/eKatastarPublic/StatisticsBasic.aspx), 2 September 2026), 11–19 GB as the service returns them (0.6–1 kB per parcel). Cadastral parcels are not among RGZ's open datasets ([data.gov.rs](https://data.gov.rs/sr/organizations/republichki-geodetski-zavod/) lists the address register, not parcels). RGZ's REST and INSPIRE WFS services for the digital cadastral plan are granted to public authorities on request to cugp@rgz.gov.rs ([REST instructions](https://geosrbija.rs/geosrbija-wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/UputstvoZaRESTv1.3.pdf), [a3.geosrbija manual](https://geosrbija.rs/geosrbija-wp/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/1.6-a3.geosrbija-_upustvo.pdf)). Bought under the fee schedule, the vector cadastral plan costs 460 dinars per parcel beyond the first hundred ([Zakon o republičkim administrativnim taksama](https://purs.gov.rs/upload/media/2025/7/3/759991/Zakon_o_republickim_administrativnim_taksama.pdf), tariff 215b, consolidated text in force from 1 July 2025), about 8.7 billion dinars for the country. Copying it through the public search would extract the whole database, which its maker may prohibit ([Zakon o autorskom i srodnim pravima, art. 138](https://www.parlament.gov.rs/upload/archive/files/lat/pdf/zakoni/2019/225-19%20-%20Lat.pdf), as amended in 2019), and the copy would age as parcels are divided and merged. What the app does instead: every device keeps what its user has seen (cells, saved parcels and their neighbourhoods). A shared server copy, or a licence, needs an agreement with RGZ.

## Recovering an imprecise browser location
The app requests both a high-accuracy watch and a fresh one-shot reading (`maximumAge: 0`). If it receives no usable fix, it restarts acquisition twice at 35-second intervals; the watch remains available afterwards. “Ponovi lociranje” starts another user-requested acquisition. Going into the background suspends the watch, and returning resumes it if GPS was not manually stopped. Generation checks discard callbacks from old sessions, and timestamp checks discard out-of-order readings. A one-shot timeout does not hide a fresh accurate watch fix. Browser-reported accuracy and timestamps are preserved; these recovery steps cannot force a device to deliver a precise fix.

Granice parcela u terenskoj mapi zadržavaju sve tačke, uključujući parcele sa više od 500 temena. Neispravna geometrija pojedinačnog rezultata ne prekida prikaz ostalih parcela; mapa prijavljuje nepotpun prikaz.
